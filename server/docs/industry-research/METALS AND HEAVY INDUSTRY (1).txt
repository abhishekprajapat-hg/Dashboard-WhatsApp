METALS AND HEAVY INDUSTRY

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering iron and steel, aluminium, foundries, fabrication, casting, forging and heavy machinery manufacturing. Your source document classifies Metals & Heavy Industry under manufacturing and includes iron and steel, aluminium, fabrication and casting, and machinery manufacturing.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
The sector contains several connected business models:
Primary metals: Mining and raw materials → smelting or steelmaking → casting → rolling and finishing → industrial distribution
Fabrication and foundries: Customer drawing or RFQ → engineering → material procurement → casting, forging or fabrication → machining → inspection → dispatch
Heavy machinery: Customer requirement → engineered design → component manufacturing → assembly → testing → installation → maintenance and spare parts
Sequential process map
1. Market and demand analysis
Analyse demand from construction, infrastructure, automotive, railways, defence, energy, engineering, shipbuilding and industrial equipment.
Track commodity prices, imports, exports, government capital expenditure and customer investment cycles.
Forecast demand by metal grade, product category, dimension, geography and end-use sector.
Determine the required capacity, product mix and sales strategy.
2. Customer acquisition and opportunity generation
Acquire customers through industrial tenders, direct sales, EPC contractors, distributors, trade exhibitions, export agents and digital outreach.
Receive requests for quotation, technical specifications, drawings or bills of quantities.
Qualify opportunities based on order volume, technical capability, delivery schedule, credit risk and expected margin.
Record the opportunity in a CRM or tender-management system.
3. Technical requirement assessment
Review material grade, dimensions, tolerances, mechanical properties, operating conditions and inspection requirements.
Determine whether the company has the required machinery, tooling, metallurgical expertise and capacity.
Identify applicable standards such as BIS, ASTM, API, ASME, ISO or customer-specific specifications.
Clarify documentation, testing and traceability expectations.
4. Product engineering and process design
Prepare drawings, manufacturing routes, process sheets and bills of materials.
Select raw materials, furnace route, casting method, forming process, machining operations and heat treatment.
Calculate process allowances, expected yield and scrap.
Define inspection stages and acceptance criteria.
5. Cost estimation and quotation
Estimate the cost of ore, scrap, alloys, energy, electrodes, refractories, labour, machining, tooling, testing and freight.
Include process yield, rejection risk, overhead, finance cost and margin.
Prepare a quotation by tonne, piece, machine, project or milestone.
Define price-validity periods and commodity-linked adjustment clauses.
6. Commercial negotiation and order confirmation
Negotiate price, quantity, technical scope, payment terms, delivery schedule and penalties.
Clarify responsibility for tooling, design changes, testing and transportation.
Receive a purchase order or signed contract.
Collect an advance where the order is customised or capital-intensive.
7. Production and capacity planning
Convert confirmed orders and forecasts into production plans.
Reserve furnace, rolling, casting, forging, machining and assembly capacity.
Sequence orders based on material grade, delivery urgency, batch size and equipment availability.
Plan labour, maintenance windows and outsourced operations.
8. Raw-material planning
Depending on the product, raw materials may include:
Iron ore
Coking coal
Limestone
Scrap metal
Direct-reduced iron
Ferroalloys
Aluminium ingots
Bauxite-derived alumina
Copper and nickel
Refractories
Industrial gases
Welding consumables
Material requirements are calculated using the bill of materials, expected yield and safety-stock rules.
9. Supplier sourcing and procurement
Issue requests for quotation to approved suppliers.
Evaluate price, grade, chemical composition, source, delivery reliability and certification.
Place purchase orders and delivery schedules.
Track imported materials, exchange-rate exposure and shipment status.
10. Material receiving and verification
Weigh incoming raw materials through a weighbridge.
Verify supplier documents, test certificates and purchase-order details.
Sample and test chemical composition, size, moisture or contamination as applicable.
Accept, quarantine or reject the shipment.
Assign batch, heat or lot numbers.
11. Raw-material storage and handling
Store ore, coal, scrap, billets, slabs, ingots, alloys and consumables in designated areas.
Prevent grade mixing and contamination.
Track stock by location, batch and weight.
Move material to production using cranes, conveyors, loaders or automated handling systems.
12. Material preparation
Depending on the plant, this stage may include:
Crushing
Screening
Beneficiation
Sintering
Pelletisation
Scrap sorting
Charge preparation
Alloy weighing
Preheating
The objective is to prepare a consistent furnace charge and improve process yield.
13. Smelting or steelmaking
For steel, common routes include:
Blast furnace and basic oxygen furnace
Electric arc furnace
Induction furnace
Direct-reduced iron followed by melting
For aluminium:
Alumina refining
Electrolytic smelting
Secondary aluminium recycling and melting
Operators control temperature, chemistry, energy usage and furnace time.
14. Metallurgical refining
Remove impurities and adjust chemical composition.
Add ferroalloys or other elements to achieve the specified grade.
Conduct ladle treatment, degassing or desulphurisation where required.
Test molten metal before casting.
15. Casting
Molten metal is converted into:
Slabs
Billets
Blooms
Ingots
Continuous-cast products
Sand castings
Investment castings
Die castings
The plant controls mould condition, pouring temperature, cooling rate and solidification.
16. Forming and primary shaping
Depending on the final product, operations may include:
Hot rolling
Cold rolling
Forging
Extrusion
Drawing
Pressing
Bending
Section forming
The objective is to achieve the required shape, thickness and mechanical properties.
17. Heat treatment
Heat, hold and cool the product according to a defined cycle.
Conduct annealing, normalising, tempering, hardening or solution treatment.
Record furnace temperature and duration.
Verify that required strength, hardness and microstructure have been achieved.
18. Fabrication and welding
For fabricated structures and machinery:
Cut plates, pipes and sections.
Form and bend parts.
Fit components using jigs and fixtures.
Perform manual, robotic or submerged-arc welding.
Inspect weld quality before further processing.
19. Machining
Perform turning, milling, boring, drilling, grinding or CNC machining.
Achieve the required dimensions, geometry and surface finish.
Track tool usage, machine time and dimensional inspection results.
Rework or reject non-conforming parts.
20. Surface treatment and finishing
Operations may include:
Shot blasting
Pickling
Galvanising
Electroplating
Painting
Powder coating
Polishing
Protective coating
The process protects the product from corrosion and improves appearance or performance.
21. Subassembly and machinery assembly
For heavy equipment:
Assemble frames, shafts, gearboxes, motors, hydraulic systems, controls and electrical panels.
Complete alignment, fastening, piping and wiring.
Integrate purchased components with manufactured parts.
Record component serial numbers and assembly history.
22. In-process quality control
Test dimensions, chemistry, hardness, tensile strength and surface condition.
Inspect welds and castings.
Verify process parameters against approved plans.
Isolate material that fails inspection.
Initiate corrective action and root-cause investigation.
23. Non-destructive testing
Where required, use:
Ultrasonic testing
Radiographic testing
Magnetic-particle inspection
Dye-penetrant inspection
Eddy-current testing
Visual inspection
These methods identify cracks, inclusions, porosity and internal defects without destroying the product.
24. Final inspection and testing
Verify final dimensions and product grade.
Conduct mechanical, chemical, pressure, load or performance tests.
Review documentation and traceability.
Arrange third-party inspection where required.
Issue material test certificates and inspection reports.
25. Packing and finished-goods storage
Bundle, wrap, crate or protect products according to transport requirements.
Mark product grade, size, heat number, customer and destination.
Store finished products by batch, project or dispatch priority.
Protect high-value machined or coated components from damage.
26. Dispatch planning and logistics
Select road, rail, sea or multimodal transport.
Plan special trailers, cranes or route permissions for oversized equipment.
Generate invoices, e-way bills, packing lists and export documents.
Track dispatch, transit status and proof of delivery.
27. Site installation and commissioning
For heavy machinery and plant equipment:
Deliver equipment to the customer site.
Install foundations, structures, mechanical equipment and controls.
Perform alignment, calibration and safety checks.
Conduct trial runs and performance testing.
Obtain customer acceptance.
28. Billing and payment collection
Payments may be linked to:
Advance
Drawing approval
Material procurement
Production completion
Dispatch
Installation
Commissioning
Performance acceptance
The finance team reconciles invoices, retention amounts, deductions, taxes and overdue receivables.
29. Warranty and field service
Respond to breakdowns, quality issues or performance complaints.
Inspect the affected component or machine.
Repair, replace or recondition the product.
Recover costs from suppliers where applicable.
Record field failures for engineering improvement.
30. Spare parts and annual maintenance
Forecast wear-part and replacement-component demand.
Supply bearings, gears, rollers, linings, seals, tools and consumables.
Offer preventive maintenance, annual maintenance contracts and machine upgrades.
Maintain installed-base and service-history records.
31. Customer retention and account expansion
Track future plant expansions and maintenance cycles.
Offer higher-value grades, engineered products, automation upgrades or energy-efficiency services.
Develop long-term rate contracts.
Cross-sell fabrication, machining, installation and after-sales support.
Measure customer profitability and repeat-order frequency.

3. Core Pain Points & Revenue Leaks
1. Raw-material and commodity-price volatility
Steel scrap, iron ore, coal, ferroalloys, aluminium, copper, nickel and energy prices can change significantly between quotation and production.
Revenue leaks
Orders accepted at outdated raw-material rates
Margin erosion during long delivery cycles
Excess inventory purchased at market peaks
Emergency procurement at premium prices
Exchange-rate losses on imported inputs
Incorrect alloy and scrap mix
Recommended controls
Commodity-linked pricing clauses
Quote-validity periods
Live price-variance dashboards
Contracted sourcing for critical inputs
Scrap and charge-mix optimisation
Customer and order-level margin tracking

2. Low yield, scrap and rework
Metal processing naturally involves yield loss. Poor process control can significantly increase melting loss, scale loss, trimming, machining allowance and rejected production.
Revenue leaks
Excess raw-material consumption
Higher energy use per accepted tonne
Recasting and reprocessing
Excess machining time
Scrap sold below recoverable value
Customer rejection
Delayed delivery
Recommended controls
Yield reporting by heat, grade and product
Digital scrap classification
Process-parameter monitoring
Statistical process control
Nesting and cutting optimisation
Root-cause analysis by machine, tool and shift

3. Equipment breakdown and bottleneck downtime
Blast furnaces, electric furnaces, rolling mills, presses, CNC machines and cranes are expensive, production-critical assets.
Revenue leaks
Lost production capacity
Idle labour
Missed customer deadlines
Emergency repair costs
Overtime recovery
Higher energy consumption after inefficient restarts
Damage to work-in-progress

Critical-asset ranking
Preventive and predictive maintenance
Condition monitoring
Digital lubrication schedules
Spare-parts criticality analysis
Overall equipment effectiveness dashboards

4. Quality failures and weak traceability
Customers may reject products due to incorrect chemistry, dimensions, mechanical properties, weld quality, internal defects or incomplete documentation.

Rework and remelting
Third-party inspection failure

Warranty claims
Loss of approved-vendor status
Inability to identify affected products quickly

Digital material test certificates
Laboratory and QMS integration
Automated dimensional inspection
NDT result storage
Supplier-lot traceability
Controlled engineering revisions

Metal production is highly energy-intensive. Furnaces, rolling mills, heat-treatment systems, compressors and pollution-control equipment materially affect profitability.
Revenue leaks

Furnace heat loss

Peak-demand penalties
Pollution-control downtime

Waste-heat recovery

Continuous emissions monitoring
Renewable power sourcing
Carbon accounting by product and customer

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue trends
India’s metals and heavy-industry outlook remains positive, supported by infrastructure, construction, railways, power, renewable energy, automotive production and industrial capital expenditure.
India produced approximately 168.4 million tonnes of crude steel in FY2025–26, representing growth of more than 10.7% year on year. Finished-steel consumption reached approximately 164 million tonnes, supported by infrastructure, construction, railways and manufacturing demand. (Press Information Bureau)
In the first quarter of FY2026–27, crude-steel production reached approximately 42.1 million tonnes, up 3% from the corresponding prior-year period, while finished-steel production grew by approximately 4.9%. India’s annual crude-steel capacity was around 222 million tonnes. (Press Information Bureau)
India remains a major global producer across multiple metals. It is the world’s second-largest aluminium producer and fourth-largest iron-ore producer. Domestic copper consumption is expected to grow strongly, driven by electricity networks, renewable energy, EVs and urban infrastructure. (IBEF)

Policy and investment outlook
India’s specialty-steel PLI programme had supported approximately ₹23,022 crore of realised investment, 2.4 million tonnes of production and more than 13,000 direct jobs by May 2026. The programme had also created 24 million tonnes of specialty-steel capacity. (Press Information Bureau)
A further round announced in November 2025 covered 85 applications and proposed approximately ₹11,887 crore of investment, with around 8.29 million tonnes of committed capacity. Incentive disbursement for these projects was scheduled to begin from FY2026–27. (Press Information Bureau)
The sector’s long-term expansion target extends beyond 2030. Government planning references approximately 500 million tonnes of steelmaking capacity by 2047, alongside efforts to reduce emissions and increase green-steel production. (Press Information Bureau)
