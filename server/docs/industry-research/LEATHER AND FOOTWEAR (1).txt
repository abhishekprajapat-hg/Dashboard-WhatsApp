LEATHER AND FOOTWEAR

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering leather tanning and finishing, leather footwear, non-leather footwear, footwear components, leather garments, bags, belts, wallets, saddlery, accessories, contract manufacturing, domestic retail and exports. Your source document classifies Leather & Footwear under manufacturing and includes leather processing, footwear manufacturing and accessories.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
The sector operates through several connected value chains:
Leather processing: Raw hides and skins → preservation → tanning → dyeing and finishing → grading → footwear, garment and accessories manufacturers
Footwear manufacturing: Market research or buyer enquiry → design → material sourcing → cutting → stitching → lasting → sole attachment → finishing → distribution
Leather goods: Customer brief → product design → pattern development → cutting → assembly → finishing → quality inspection → retail or export delivery
Private-label export manufacturing: International buyer enquiry → sample development → compliance approval → commercial order → production → inspection → export → repeat-order management
Sequential process map
1. Market and trend analysis
Analyse consumer preferences, fashion trends, seasonal demand, sports and lifestyle trends, formal and casual footwear demand and regional buying behaviour.
Review competitor products, pricing, materials, colours, designs and distribution channels.
Track demand from domestic retail, institutional buyers, global brands and export markets.
Identify opportunities in leather footwear, sports shoes, safety footwear, sandals, bags, belts, wallets, garments and sustainable alternatives.
Forecast expected demand by product category, gender, size, colour, geography and price band.
2. Customer acquisition and sales opportunity generation
Acquire customers through trade exhibitions, export councils, sourcing agents, brand outreach, distributors, retail networks, online marketplaces and direct B2B prospecting.
Receive requests for quotation, product briefs, technical packs, sample references or design specifications.
Qualify the buyer based on expected volume, target price, quality requirements, compliance standards, payment terms and delivery expectations.
Record the enquiry, samples, quotations and buyer communication in CRM.
3. Product concept and collection planning
For domestic brands:
Define collection themes, target customers, product categories and price points.

Plan the number of styles and variants.
Estimate initial and replenishment quantities.
For export and private-label manufacturers:
Review the buyer’s design brief or reference sample.
Confirm materials, construction, testing and packaging requirements.
Determine whether the product is technically and commercially feasible.
4. Product design
Create footwear, garment or accessory concepts.
Prepare sketches, digital designs, colourways and material combinations.
Design the upper, sole, lining, insole, trims and fastening system.

Prepare upper patterns, lining patterns, reinforcement pieces and cutting templates.
Develop size grading.
Create bills of materials and operation sequences.
Define stitching, edge finishing, adhesives, thread, hardware and quality tolerances.
6. Last, sole and tooling development

Select or develop the shoe last that defines shape and fit.
Design outsole, midsole, heel, insole and moulded components.
Develop dies, moulds, cutting knives and fixtures.
Confirm size range and grading.
Test compatibility between the upper and sole.

13. Hide and skin sourcing
For leather-processing businesses:
Source hides and skins from slaughterhouses, meat-processing facilities, traders and collection centres.
Assess species, size, thickness, preservation, damage and defect level.
Negotiate price based on recoverable area and expected grade.
Arrange transport to the tannery.
Record source and batch details.
14. Hide preservation
Raw hides and skins are highly perishable.
Clean visible contamination.
Apply salt or other approved preservation methods.
Control time, temperature and moisture.
Prevent bacterial damage.
Store preserved material before processing.
15. Incoming hide grading
Inspect hides for cuts, scars, holes, branding marks, disease damage and preservation defects.
Grade by size, thickness and quality.
Segregate hides by intended end use.
Record supplier quality and expected yield.
Reject severely damaged material.
16. Soaking and beamhouse processing
Leather processing may include:
Soaking

unhairing
fleshing
splitting
deliming
bating

These stages remove unwanted material, open the fibre structure and prepare the hide for tanning.
17. Tanning
Stabilise the hide structure using chrome, vegetable or alternative tanning systems.
Control pH, temperature, chemical dosage and processing time.
Produce wet blue, wet white or vegetable-tanned leather.
Test shrinkage temperature and tanning consistency.
Record chemical use and batch data.
18. Splitting and shaving
Split leather into required layers.
Shave it to uniform thickness.
Segregate grain and split material.
Measure usable area.
Track yield and waste.
19. Retanning, dyeing and fatliquoring
Adjust softness, fullness, strength and feel through retanning.
Add dyes to achieve the required shade.
Apply oils and fatliquors for flexibility.
Control drum recipe and processing parameters.
Test colour consistency and physical properties.
20. Drying and conditioning
Remove moisture using vacuum drying, toggling, hanging or other methods.
Condition the leather to restore the required moisture level.
Stretch or soften the material.
Prevent excessive shrinkage or stiffness.
Prepare leather for final finishing.
21. Leather finishing
Apply base coats, pigments, resins, waxes and protective coatings.
Emboss or print surface patterns where required.
Buff, polish or glaze the leather.
Control colour, gloss, touch and coating adhesion.
Inspect for finish defects.
22. Finished-leather grading and measurement
Inspect leather for shade, grain, finish, thickness and defects.
Grade according to usable cutting area.
Measure area electronically.
Label by batch, colour, grade and customer specification.
Pack or allocate the leather for internal production.

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue trends
India is a major global leather and footwear producer. It accounts for approximately 13% of global leather production and is the world’s second-largest producer and consumer of leather footwear. (India Brand Equity Foundation)
The sector’s exports reached approximately US$4.75 billion in FY2025–26. The government has publicly encouraged the industry to target at least US$15 billion in exports over the next five to six years, although this is an ambition rather than a guaranteed forecast. (Press Information Bureau)
Industry projections cited by the government suggest that the leather and footwear sector could reach approximately US$50 billion in production by 2030, comprising US$36 billion in domestic consumption and US$14 billion in exports. These figures should be interpreted as an industry growth scenario rather than a certainty. (Press Information Bureau)

Policy and export outlook
India has approximately 15,000 footwear manufacturing units, with a large MSME presence and major clusters in Agra, Kanpur, Chennai, Ranipet, Ambur and Kolkata. (Press Information Bureau)
The Indian Footwear and Leather Development Programme operated with an outlay of ₹1,700 crore for FY2021–22 to FY2025–26, supporting technology upgrades, environmental management, clusters, design studios and brand development. (Press Information Bureau)
The government has also simplified some export procedures. Restrictions limiting exports of several processed leather categories to specified ports were removed, and certain mandatory CLRI testing requirements for export were discontinued in 2025. (Press Information Bureau)
Future trade agreements could improve market access. For example, the India–New Zealand agreement provides for elimination of tariffs on New Zealand tariff lines covering Indian leather, leather products and footwear. (Leather Exports Council)
