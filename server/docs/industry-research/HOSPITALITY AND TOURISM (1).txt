HOSPITALITY & TOURISM

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering hotels, resorts, restaurants, cafés, serviced apartments, hostels, homestays, travel agencies, tour operators, destination-management companies, online travel platforms, event and wedding tourism, MICE, adventure tourism, pilgrimage tourism, medical tourism, wellness tourism and tourism-support services.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
Hospitality and tourism businesses create value by combining accommodation, food, transport, experiences, information, convenience and customer service.
The sector operates through several connected business models:
Hotels and resorts: Market analysis → property development or management agreement → distribution → reservation → guest stay → billing → review → repeat booking
Restaurants and food service: Concept development → menu design → procurement → food preparation → table or delivery service → payment → feedback → retention
Travel agencies: Enquiry → traveller profiling → itinerary and quotation → booking → payment → travel support → post-trip follow-up
Tour operators: Destination and package design → supplier contracting → promotion → booking → trip operations → guide and transport coordination → customer feedback
Online travel platforms: Supplier onboarding → digital catalogue → customer acquisition → search and comparison → booking → payment → settlement → service support
MICE and event tourism: Corporate or event enquiry → proposal → venue and supplier contracting → attendee management → event delivery → reconciliation → repeat business
Sequential Process Map
1. Market and destination analysis
Analyse domestic, inbound and outbound travel demand.
Study leisure, corporate, pilgrimage, medical, wellness, adventure, wedding and event travel.
Review seasonal demand, public holidays, festivals, school breaks and corporate travel cycles.
Analyse source markets by city, country, income, age and travel purpose.
Review air, rail and road connectivity.
Study existing hotels, restaurants, attractions and tour operators.
Estimate visitor spending, average stay and likely price sensitivity.
Identify underserved destinations, customer segments and experience categories.
India recorded approximately 20.22 million international tourist arrivals, 9.15 million foreign tourist arrivals and 4.29 billion domestic tourist visits in 2025, according to the Ministry of Tourism’s current dashboard. The dashboard also estimates tourism’s total contribution at 5.22% of GDP and 13.34% of employment. (Ministry of Tourism)

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue Trends
India’s hospitality and tourism sector has a favourable three-to-five-year outlook, supported by domestic travel, weddings, corporate events, religious tourism, improved transport infrastructure and growing international arrivals.
The Ministry of Tourism reported approximately 20.57 million international tourist arrivals in 2024, exceeding 2019 levels and representing an increase of around 8.9% over 2023. (Press Information Bureau)
Current 2025 dashboard figures show approximately 9.15 million foreign tourist arrivals, 11.07 million NRI arrivals and 4.29 billion domestic tourist visits. Domestic tourism remains the largest volume driver for Indian hospitality businesses. (Ministry of Tourism)
Industry estimates cited by IBEF project Indian hotel-sector revenue growth of approximately 9–12% in FY2025–26. Premium hotel demand was expected to grow faster than room supply, supporting pricing and occupancy, although actual outcomes vary by city and property. (India Brand Equity Foundation)
