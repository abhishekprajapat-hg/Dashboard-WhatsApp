FINANCIAL SERVICES

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering banking, insurance, non-banking financial companies, fintech, digital payments, lending, wealth management, investment firms, mutual funds, brokerage, pension products and financial advisory services. Your source document classifies Financial Services under the tertiary sector and includes banking, insurance, NBFCs, fintech and investment firms.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
The financial-services industry operates through several connected business models:
Banking: Customer acquisition → KYC and account opening → deposits or lending → transaction processing → servicing → risk monitoring → retention
NBFC lending: Lead generation → borrower assessment → credit underwriting → loan disbursement → repayment collection → delinquency management → repeat lending
Insurance: Customer need analysis → quotation → underwriting → policy issuance → premium collection → servicing → claims → renewal
Fintech and payments: User acquisition → digital onboarding → transaction authorisation → payment processing → settlement → fraud monitoring → retention
Investment and wealth services: Investor acquisition → risk profiling → product recommendation → investment execution → portfolio monitoring → reporting → recurring advisory

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue trends
India’s financial-services sector has a strong three-to-five-year outlook, supported by expanding formal credit, digital payments, financial inclusion, insurance adoption and household participation in investments.
Scheduled commercial banks recorded 15.9% year-on-year non-food credit growth in FY2025–26. Credit to services grew 19%, industrial credit increased 15%, and personal loans grew 16.2%, indicating broad-based borrowing demand. (Press Information Bureau)
Public-sector banks reported combined net profit of approximately ₹1.98 lakh crore in FY2025–26, while their gross NPA ratio declined to 1.93% and net NPA ratio to 0.39% as of March 31, 2026. Their total business reached ₹283.3 lakh crore. These figures indicate stronger balance sheets, although individual banks and loan segments can still carry materially different risks. (Press Information Bureau)
Financial inclusion remains a major growth foundation. As of July 1, 2026, the Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana had approximately 58.63 crore accounts with deposits exceeding ₹3.08 lakh crore. Government data also reported 59.14 crore Mudra loans amounting to ₹41.71 lakh crore. (Press Information Bureau)
