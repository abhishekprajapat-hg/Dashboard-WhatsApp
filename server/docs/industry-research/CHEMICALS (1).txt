CHEMICALS & PETROCHEMICALS

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering fertilizers, plastics, paints and coatings, industrial chemicals, bulk chemicals, specialty chemicals, petrochemical intermediates, polymers, solvents, agrochemicals and refinery-linked downstream products. Your source document classifies Chemicals & Petrochemicals under manufacturing and specifically includes fertilizers, plastics, paints and coatings, and industrial chemicals.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
The industry operates through several connected value chains:
Petrochemicals: Crude oil or natural gas processing → basic feedstocks → intermediates → polymers or chemical derivatives → industrial customers
Bulk chemicals: Raw-material procurement → chemical reaction or conversion → purification → storage → bulk dispatch
Specialty chemicals: Customer requirement → formulation or molecule development → pilot testing → regulated production → technical sales and repeat supply
Fertilizers: Feedstock sourcing → synthesis or blending → granulation → bagging → dealer and agricultural distribution
Sequential process map
1. Market and demand analysis
Analyse demand from agriculture, pharmaceuticals, textiles, automobiles, construction, packaging, paints, personal care, electronics and consumer goods.
Track crude oil, natural gas, naphtha, coal, mineral, solvent and feedstock prices.
Monitor domestic production, imports, exports, duties and regulatory changes.
Forecast demand by chemical grade, customer segment, region and application.
Identify potential import-substitution and export opportunities.
2. Customer acquisition and opportunity generation
Acquire customers through direct industrial sales, distributors, tenders, trade exhibitions, export agents, digital campaigns and technical referrals.
Receive product enquiries, technical specifications or requests for quotation.
Qualify the opportunity based on volume, application, purity, regulatory requirements, payment terms and expected margin.
Record samples, trials, approvals and commercial discussions in CRM.
3. Technical requirement assessment
Review required chemical composition, concentration, purity, viscosity, colour, particle size, performance and packaging.
Understand the customer’s manufacturing process and end application.
Identify applicable standards, safety requirements and testing methods.
Determine whether the requirement can be met using an existing product or requires new development.
4. Product development and formulation
For specialty chemicals, paints, coatings and formulated products:
Develop the chemical formulation, recipe or molecular process.
Select active ingredients, solvents, additives, catalysts, pigments and stabilisers.
Produce laboratory samples.
Test compatibility, stability, performance and safety.
Revise the formula until target specifications are achieved.
For commodity petrochemicals, this stage focuses more on:

Plant configuration
Feedstock selection
Product grade
Expected conversion and yield
5. Regulatory, environmental and safety assessment
Identify licences, registrations, environmental approvals and hazardous-chemical requirements.
Prepare safety data sheets and transport classifications.
Review waste, emissions, effluent and occupational-exposure requirements.
Determine whether the product requires specific approval for agriculture, food contact, pharmaceuticals or export.
Approve the product and process only after safety and compliance review.
6. Pilot batch and customer sampling
Produce laboratory, pilot or small commercial batches.
Test the batch against product specifications.
Send samples and technical documents to the customer.
Conduct application trials at the customer’s facility where required.
Record feedback and revise the product or process.
7. Product approval and vendor registration

Once approved, the manufacturer becomes eligible for regular orders or annual-rate contracts.
8. Costing and quotation
Calculate feedstock, solvent, catalyst, packaging, energy, labour, effluent-treatment, freight and compliance costs.
Include expected reaction yield, solvent recovery, by-product credit, waste-treatment expense and rejection risk.
Account for exchange-rate exposure where feedstocks are imported.
Quote by kilogram, litre, tonne, drum, tanker or contract batch.
Include price-adjustment provisions where feedstock prices are volatile.
9. Commercial negotiation and order confirmation
Negotiate price, volume, minimum batch, delivery schedule, technical specification and credit terms.
Agree on testing, certificate-of-analysis and rejection conditions.
Confirm responsibility for transport, hazardous-goods handling and insurance.
Receive purchase order, supply contract or annual-rate agreement.

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue trends
India’s chemicals and petrochemicals sector remains a major part of the manufacturing economy. It contributed 8.1% of manufacturing gross value added in FY2023–24. Production of selected major chemicals and petrochemicals increased from 45.638 million tonnes in FY2015–16 to 58.617 million tonnes in FY2024–25, representing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 2.8%.
The industry is highly diversified, covering bulk chemicals, specialty chemicals, agrochemicals, polymers, petrochemicals and fertilizers. India is reported as the world’s sixth-largest chemical producer and third-largest in Asia.
Government and industry estimates placed the sector’s value at approximately US$220 billion in 2026, with an aspiration to reach around US$300 billion by 2030, US$1 trillion by 2040 and US$2 trillion by 2047. These figures should be understood as strategic growth ambitions rather than guaranteed forecasts.
