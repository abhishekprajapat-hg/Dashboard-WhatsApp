AUTOMOBILE & Auto components 


The following is an India-focused industry audit covering passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, two-wheelers, three-wheelers, electric vehicles, automotive components, OEM suppliers, dealerships, servicing and the replacement-parts market. Your sector document classifies Automobile & Auto Components under manufacturing and includes passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, auto-parts manufacturing and EVs.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
The industry operates through two connected value chains:
Vehicle manufacturing: Market demand → vehicle design → component sourcing → assembly → dealer distribution → retail sale → service and retention
Auto-component manufacturing: OEM enquiry → technical development → supplier approval → tooling → component production → quality validation → OEM delivery → aftermarket sales
Sequential process map
1. Market research and demand forecasting
Analyse vehicle registrations, customer preferences, fuel prices, financing availability, government policies and competitor launches.
Forecast demand by vehicle model, fuel type, geography, variant and sales channel.
Identify opportunities in passenger vehicles, commercial fleets, two-wheelers, EVs and replacement parts.
Translate demand forecasts into product, production and capacity plans.
2. Product and platform strategy
Decide whether to develop a new vehicle, modify an existing platform or introduce a new variant.
Define the target price, performance, range, safety, payload, emissions and customer segment.
Establish the make-or-buy strategy for major systems.
Prepare the target bill of materials and expected product margin.
3. Vehicle and component design
Create digital designs for the body, chassis, powertrain, battery, electronics, interior and safety systems.
Perform engineering simulations for strength, aerodynamics, heat, vibration, crash safety and battery performance.
Develop detailed component specifications and engineering drawings.
Maintain approved design versions and engineering-change records.
4. Prototype development and validation
Build digital and physical prototypes.
Test performance, durability, braking, crashworthiness, emissions, noise, vibration, thermal behaviour and electrical safety.
For EVs, test battery range, charging, thermal management, battery safety and software performance.
Modify the design based on testing results.
5. Regulatory approval and homologation
Submit the vehicle or component for applicable testing and certification.
Verify compliance with safety, emissions, dimensions, lighting, braking and environmental requirements.
Complete type approval before commercial production.
Maintain compliance documentation for future audits and model changes.
6. Supplier identification and nomination
Identify Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers.
Issue requests for quotation containing technical specifications, expected volumes and quality requirements.
Evaluate suppliers on cost, capacity, technology, financial stability, quality performance and delivery reliability.
Nominate approved suppliers and finalise commercial terms.
7. Component development and approval
Suppliers develop prototypes, production tools, dies, moulds, fixtures and inspection gauges.
Conduct design reviews and production trials.
Validate dimensions, materials, durability and performance.
Complete Production Part Approval Process documentation before regular supply.
8. Procurement and material planning
Convert production forecasts and confirmed demand into component and material requirements.
Order steel, aluminium, plastics, rubber, electronics, batteries, motors, castings and finished components.
Set delivery schedules according to production sequence.
Track supplier confirmations and shortages.
9. Inbound logistics
Collect materials and components from suppliers.
Consolidate shipments where practical.
Deliver parts to the plant according to just-in-time or just-in-sequence requirements.
Scan and verify material at the receiving dock.
10. Incoming quality control
Inspect received material for dimensions, composition, damage and functional performance.
Match the component against the approved specification and supplier batch.
Accept, quarantine, reject or return non-conforming material.
Record supplier quality incidents and corrective actions.
11. Raw-material and component storage
Store material according to production requirements.
Maintain location, lot and serial-number visibility.
Protect sensitive electronics, batteries, rubber and painted components.
Replenish production lines through controlled material movement.
12. Component manufacturing
Depending on the supplier, this may include:
Casting
Forging
Machining
Stamping
Welding
Injection moulding
Rubber moulding
Heat treatment
Surface treatment
Painting
Electronics assembly
Battery-pack assembly
Production data is recorded by machine, batch, tool, operator and shift.
13. Subassembly production
Assemble engines, transmissions, axles, seats, instrument panels, wiring harnesses, battery packs and other modules.
Conduct functional and leak testing.
Assign serial numbers to critical subassemblies.
Deliver modules to the final assembly line in sequence.
14. Vehicle body manufacturing
Stamp sheet-metal panels.
Weld panels into the body structure.
Inspect weld strength and dimensional accuracy.
Transfer the body to the paint shop.
15. Painting and surface treatment
Clean and prepare the vehicle body.
Apply anti-corrosion treatment, primer, base coat and clear coat.
Cure paint through controlled ovens.
Inspect colour, thickness, finish and surface defects.
16. Final vehicle assembly
Install powertrain, suspension, braking systems, wiring, electronics, interior, glass, wheels and exterior components.
For EVs, install the battery pack, electric motor, inverter, charging equipment and thermal-management system.
Complete software configuration and electronic control unit programming.
Track each vehicle through its unique identification number.
17. In-process quality assurance
Inspect critical assembly stages.
Verify torque values, electrical connections, fluid levels and component fitment.
Use error-proofing systems to prevent incorrect parts or missed operations.
Stop the line or isolate the vehicle when a defect is detected.
18. End-of-line inspection and testing
Conduct brake, wheel-alignment, headlamp, emissions, electrical and diagnostic tests.
Inspect water leakage, noise, paint finish and cabin quality.
Perform road or roller testing.
Approve, repair or hold the finished vehicle.
19. Finished-vehicle inventory
Park approved vehicles in the finished-goods yard.
Allocate vehicles to dealers, institutional buyers, fleet customers or export orders.
Track inventory by model, variant, colour and location.
Monitor ageing stock and dispatch priority.
20. Dealer and distributor allocation
Allocate vehicles according to dealer demand, sales forecasts and market priorities.
Generate dispatch instructions and commercial documentation.
Provide dealers with pricing, schemes, product information and sales targets.
Track dealer stock and retail conversion.
21. Outbound logistics
Load vehicles onto specialised carriers or rail transport.
Deliver auto components through road, rail, air or sea freight.
Monitor route, estimated arrival and transit damage.
Capture proof of delivery.
22. Marketing and lead generation
Generate enquiries through dealerships, websites, marketplaces, roadshows, fleet partnerships and digital advertising.
Capture customer details and preferred vehicle specifications.
Assign enquiries to sales representatives or dealers.
Track campaign source and conversion performance.
23. Customer qualification and test drive
Understand the buyer’s purpose, budget, vehicle preference and financing requirement.
Arrange a test drive.
Evaluate trade-in requirements where applicable.
Present suitable variants, ownership costs and available offers.
24. Quotation, finance and insurance
Provide an on-road quotation.
Arrange loan eligibility checks and vehicle-finance applications.
Offer insurance, extended warranty, accessories and service packages.
Negotiate discounts, delivery dates and exchange value.
25. Booking and order confirmation
Collect the booking amount.
Reserve or order the required vehicle.
Verify customer identity, loan approval and registration documents.
Communicate delivery expectations.
26. Registration and pre-delivery inspection
Complete invoicing, insurance and vehicle registration.
Conduct a pre-delivery inspection.
Install approved accessories.
Prepare the vehicle for customer handover.
27. Vehicle delivery and payment collection
Collect the balance payment or financier disbursement.
Explain vehicle controls, warranty, servicing and roadside assistance.
Obtain customer acknowledgement and delivery confirmation.
Activate connected-vehicle and service accounts where applicable.
28. Warranty and after-sales service
Schedule periodic servicing.
Diagnose mechanical, electrical or software problems.
Process warranty claims.
Maintain service and repair history.
Manage parts availability and technician allocation.
29. Spare-parts and aftermarket operations
Forecast replacement-part demand.
Supply parts to authorised service centres, independent workshops, retailers and distributors.
Manage genuine parts, accessories, lubricants and remanufactured components.
Identify counterfeit or unauthorised supply channels.
30. Recall and field-quality management
Analyse warranty, breakdown and customer complaint data.
Trace affected parts, suppliers and vehicle identification numbers.
Inform dealers and vehicle owners.
Replace or repair affected components and close the recall record.
31. Customer retention and lifecycle revenue
Send service reminders, insurance-renewal notices and warranty-expiry alerts.
Offer accessories, service packages and extended warranties.
Encourage upgrades, exchanges and repeat purchases.
Measure customer satisfaction, service retention and lifetime value.

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue trends
India’s automobile and component ecosystem remains one of the country’s largest manufacturing industries. ACMA reports that the automotive industry is valued above US$180 billion, while the auto-component segment reached approximately US$80.2 billion in FY2024–25, representing year-on-year growth of 9.6%. Component sales to OEMs reached US$67.9 billion, exports reached US$22.9 billion and the domestic aftermarket reached US$11.8 billion. (ACMA)
The three-to-five-year outlook will be supported by:
Rising vehicle ownership
Replacement demand
Infrastructure and freight growth
Premiumisation of passenger vehicles
EV adoption
Increased electronic content per vehicle
Export and global sourcing opportunities
Growth of the organised aftermarket
Domestic battery and component localisation
Software-defined and connected vehicles
Growth will not be uniform across every segment. Internal-combustion component suppliers, particularly those concentrated in engine and transmission parts, may face slower long-term demand unless they diversify into EV, electronics, lightweighting or aftermarket products.

India’s PLI-Auto programme had generated cumulative investment of approximately ₹44,326 crore, incremental sales of ₹52,414 crore and 67,820 jobs by March 31, 2026. The scheme requires at least 50% domestic value addition for eligible advanced automotive products. (Press Information Bureau)
The separate Advanced Chemistry Cell programme has a ₹18,100 crore outlay and targets 50 GWh of domestic battery capacity. As of May 31, 2026, beneficiary firms had reported investment of ₹5,180 crore, although no incentive had yet been claimed under the programme. (Press Information Bureau)

Government and investment outlook
The Government of India’s PLI-Auto programme has a total budgetary outlay of ₹25,938 crore and is designed to encourage domestic manufacturing of advanced automotive technology products. By March 31, 2026, 225 manufacturing units had been established under approved applicants across India. (Press Information Bureau)
As of July 16, 2026, 18 applicants had received domestic-value-addition certificates covering 154 products or variants. This indicates that localisation is moving beyond investment announcements into certified products, although the pace differs by company and technology. (Press Information Bureau)
The policy environment supports advanced components, EVs and battery localisation, but long-term commercial viability will still depend on market demand, product quality, cost competitiveness and technology execution.
