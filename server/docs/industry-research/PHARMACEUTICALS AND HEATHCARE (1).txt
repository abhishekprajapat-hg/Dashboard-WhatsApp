PHARMACEUTICALS AND HEATHCARE

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering pharmaceutical formulations, active pharmaceutical ingredients, vaccines, biologics, medical devices, contract manufacturing, quality control, regulated distribution and post-market surveillance. Your source document classifies Pharmaceuticals & Healthcare Manufacturing under manufacturing and includes medicines, vaccines, APIs and medical devices.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
The sector operates through several connected value chains:
Finished medicines: Research or product selection → formulation development → regulatory approval → commercial manufacturing → distribution → pharmacovigilance
APIs and bulk drugs: Chemical-route development → raw-material sourcing → synthesis → purification → quality release → formulation manufacturers
Vaccines and biologics: Cell-bank development → biological production → purification → sterile filling → cold-chain distribution
Medical devices: Clinical need identification → engineering design → prototyping → validation → regulated manufacturing → installation and service
Sequential process map
1. Market and therapeutic-area analysis
Analyse disease patterns, treatment demand, prescribing behaviour, hospital requirements and public-health programmes.
Review patent expiries, competitor products, import dependence and export opportunities.
Identify opportunities in generic medicines, complex generics, vaccines, biosimilars, APIs and medical devices.
Estimate market size, target price, development cost and potential product lifecycle.
2. Product portfolio selection
Select products based on therapeutic demand, technical capability, regulatory complexity and expected margin.
Decide whether the business will develop:
Generic formulations
Branded generics
APIs
Vaccines
Biosimilars
Medical devices
Contract-development or manufacturing services
Assess whether the product can be manufactured internally or should be outsourced.
3. Research and development
For pharmaceutical products:
Develop the active ingredient or formulation.
Select excipients, dosage form, strength and manufacturing process.
Conduct pre-formulation, analytical and stability studies.
Develop test methods and product specifications.
For medical devices:
Define clinical and user requirements.
Develop mechanical, electrical, software and material specifications.
Assess usability, safety and expected device performance.
4. Intellectual-property and freedom-to-operate review
Review relevant patents and market-exclusivity periods.
Determine whether the proposed product or process may infringe existing rights.
Identify alternative formulations, processes or device designs.
Plan the product launch around regulatory and patent timelines.
5. Feasibility and development costing
Estimate laboratory, clinical, tooling, validation and regulatory costs.
Calculate expected raw-material, packaging, quality, labour and utility expenses.
Estimate market price, margin and break-even volume.
Approve, revise or discontinue the development project.
6. Laboratory development
For medicines and APIs:
Conduct small-scale synthesis or formulation trials.
Optimise reaction conditions, mixing, granulation, compression, coating or filling.
Develop analytical methods.
Establish preliminary product specifications.
For devices:
Build engineering prototypes.
Test materials, sensors, electronics, software and mechanical performance.
Refine the design based on test results.
7. Preclinical and clinical evaluation
Depending on the product and regulatory pathway:
Conduct laboratory and animal studies.
Assess toxicity, pharmacology or biocompatibility.
Prepare clinical-trial protocols.
Recruit approved clinical sites and participants.
Collect and analyse safety and efficacy data.
Generic products may require bioequivalence studies rather than full clinical trials, while some devices require clinical-performance or usability evidence.
8. Regulatory strategy and submission
Identify the appropriate approval pathway for India and target export markets.
Compile chemistry, manufacturing, quality, safety and clinical information.
Submit dossiers, licence applications and supporting documents.
Respond to regulator questions and inspection findings.
Track approval status and commitments.
9. Technology transfer
Transfer the approved laboratory process to pilot and commercial manufacturing.
Prepare manufacturing instructions, master formulas and analytical methods.
Train production and quality teams.
Conduct engineering, exhibit or validation batches.
Resolve differences between laboratory and plant performance.
10. Supplier identification and qualification
Potential suppliers include:
API manufacturers
Excipient suppliers
Chemical and solvent suppliers
Primary-packaging suppliers
Device-component manufacturers
Contract laboratories
Cold-chain logistics providers
The manufacturer evaluates:
Regulatory status

Delivery reliability
Business-continuity risk
11. Raw-material and packaging procurement
Calculate requirements using forecasts, confirmed orders and safety stock.
Issue purchase orders only to approved suppliers.
Track long-lead and imported materials.
Monitor expiry, storage requirements and regulatory documentation.
Maintain alternatives for critical materials where possible.
12. Material receipt and quarantine
Receive raw materials, components and packaging.
Verify supplier, purchase order, container condition and quantity.
Assign an internal material or lot number.
Place material in quarantine until quality approval.
Store samples and documents for testing.
13. Incoming quality control
Sample materials according to approved procedures.
Test identity, purity, potency, moisture, microbial quality or physical attributes.
Review supplier certificates against internal results.
Approve, reject or hold the material.
Record any supplier deviation or investigation.
14. Controlled material storage
Store materials according to labelled requirements.
Maintain temperature, humidity, light and security controls.
Segregate approved, quarantined, rejected and returned materials.
Apply FEFO inventory practices.
Use additional controls for narcotic, hazardous or high-potency substances.
15. Production planning and batch scheduling
Convert forecasts and orders into manufacturing schedules.
Reserve production lines, equipment, operators and quality resources.
Sequence batches to reduce cleaning requirements and cross-contamination risk.
Coordinate material availability, testing and packaging-line capacity.
Plan preventive maintenance and calibration.
16. Dispensing and material issuance
Issue approved materials against a specific batch record.
Verify identity, lot number and required quantity.
Weigh ingredients in controlled dispensing areas.
Record actual quantities and reconcile any difference.
Prevent incorrect material selection through independent checks or barcode verification.

24. Finished-product quality testing
Test identity, potency, purity, dissolution, sterility, microbial quality and stability as applicable.
Verify packaging and label accuracy.
Review analytical results and manufacturing documentation.
Investigate out-of-specification or unusual results.
Retain product samples for future reference.
25. Batch record review and release
Review manufacturing, packaging, testing and deviation records.
Confirm that all approved procedures were followed.
Ensure investigations and corrective actions are complete.
Authorised quality personnel approve or reject the batch.
Only released batches may enter saleable inventory.
26. Finished-goods warehousing
Store products under approved conditions.
Track inventory by batch, expiry date, location and customer.
Maintain cold storage for vaccines, biologics and temperature-sensitive medicines.
Prevent shipment of quarantined or recalled products.
Prioritise dispatch according to FEFO.

Select the correct storage and transport conditions.
Pack products using qualified shipping systems where required.
Monitor location and temperature.
Capture delivery evidence and temperature excursions.
29. Billing and payment collection
Generate invoices after dispatch or delivery.
Reconcile contract price, taxes, freight and quantities.
Track distributor credit and institutional receivables.
Manage government-tender payments and deductions.
Follow up overdue accounts.
30. Product complaints and quality investigation
Record complaints by product, batch and market.
Collect samples, photographs and supporting information.
Review batch, laboratory, storage and transport records.
Determine whether the issue is product-related, distribution-related or usage-related.
Implement corrective and preventive action.
31. Pharmacovigilance and device vigilance
For medicines:
Collect adverse-event reports.
Evaluate seriousness and expectedness.
Identify potential safety signals.
Submit required reports to regulators.
Update warnings or prescribing information where necessary.
For devices:
Monitor malfunctions, injuries and performance failures.
Evaluate whether field action is required.
Track corrective actions and customer communication.
32. Recall and field-correction management
Identify affected batches, serial numbers and customers.
Stop further distribution.
Notify regulators, distributors and healthcare providers.
Recover, replace or correct affected products.
Reconcile recovered quantities.
Investigate the root cause and effectiveness of the recall.
33. Post-market and stability monitoring
Continue product-stability studies.
Review recurring complaints and adverse-event trends.
Analyse market performance by batch and geography.
Update product specifications where justified.
Maintain regulatory licences and product registrations.
34. Customer retention and portfolio expansion
Maintain reliable supply and service levels.
Support hospitals, distributors and healthcare professionals.
Develop new dosage strengths, pack sizes and delivery formats.
Offer contract development, analytical testing or manufacturing services.
Expand approved products into additional countries.
Cross-sell related medicines, devices and support services.

5. Future Growth Prospects
Revenue trends
India’s pharmaceutical industry remains one of the country’s strongest manufacturing and export sectors. In FY2024–25, industry turnover reached approximately ₹4.72 lakh crore. India ranks third globally by pharmaceutical production volume and supplies about 20% of global generic-medicine demand. (Press Information Bureau)
The industry was valued at around US$60 billion in 2026, with public and industry projections indicating potential growth to approximately US$130 billion by 2030. This should be treated as a strategic projection rather than a guaranteed outcome. (Press Information Bureau)
Pharmaceutical exports reached approximately US$30.5 billion in FY2024–25, and exports for FY2025–26 subsequently crossed US$31 billion. India exports medicines to more than 190 countries, with a substantial proportion going to highly regulated markets. (Press Information Bureau)

India’s medical-device exports increased from approximately US$2.5 billion in FY2020–21 to US$4.1 billion in FY2024–25, with exports reaching 187 countries. (Press Information Bureau)

Policy and investment outlook
Government programmes continue to encourage pharmaceutical, API and medical-device manufacturing.
Under the pharmaceutical PLI programme, cumulative sales had reached approximately ₹3.35 lakh crore by December 2025, including around ₹2.15 lakh crore in exports. (Press Information Bureau)
The bulk-drug programme had approved 48 greenfield projects by December 2025, with actual investment of approximately ₹4,814 crore and 38 commissioned projects. (Press Information Bureau)
Government-supported bulk-drug parks in Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh were also under development, with grants approved for shared infrastructure. (Press Information Bureau)
These programmes improve domestic capacity, but sustained success will still depend on quality compliance, commercial viability, environmental management and international competitiveness.
