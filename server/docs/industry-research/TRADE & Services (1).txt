TRADE & COMMERCE

The following is an India-focused industry audit covering offline retail, e-commerce, wholesale trade, distribution, direct-to-consumer brands, marketplaces, omnichannel commerce, general trade, modern trade, institutional sales and government procurement. Your source document classifies Trade & Commerce under the tertiary sector and includes offline retail, e-commerce, wholesale trade and distribution.
1. End-to-End Business Flow
Trade and commerce businesses connect manufacturers, brands and importers with business or end customers. The sector does not usually transform raw materials substantially. Its commercial value comes from:
Product sourcing
Assortment selection
Market access
Inventory availability
Distribution reach
Merchandising
Pricing
Convenience
Customer experience
Credit
Fulfilment
After-sales support
The sector operates through several connected business models:
Offline retail: Supplier sourcing → merchandise planning → store inventory → customer purchase → payment → fulfilment → returns and loyalty
E-commerce: Seller or brand onboarding → catalogue listing → digital acquisition → online order → payment → fulfilment → delivery → returns and repeat purchase
Wholesale trade: Supplier contracting → bulk procurement → warehousing → retailer or institutional order → dispatch → credit collection → repeat supply
Distribution: Brand appointment → territory planning → stock purchase → secondary sales → retailer servicing → claims and collections
Direct-to-consumer commerce: Product development or sourcing → digital marketing → website order → fulfilment → customer support → retention

3. Core Pain Points & Revenue Leaks
1. Excess inventory, stockouts and poor forecasting
Trade businesses must buy stock before knowing final customer demand. Forecasting errors create excess stock in one product or location while another product remains unavailable.
Revenue leaks
Lost sales due to stockouts
capital tied up in inventory
markdowns
expired products
obsolete models
excess warehouse space
supplier cancellation charges
emergency transfers and freight
weak customer experience
Recommended controls
SKU-location demand forecasting
store and warehouse replenishment rules

minimum and maximum stock

seasonal forecasts
real-time sell-through
smaller initial buys and rapid replenishment
2. Margin leakage through discounts, commissions and hidden channel costs
A product may appear profitable when measured only as selling price minus purchase price. The true margin may be much lower after discounts, marketplace commission, delivery, payment fees, returns and marketing.
